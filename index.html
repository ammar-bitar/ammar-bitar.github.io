Agents and knowledge graphs for science

A multi-agent reviewer scientists across the institute use on real manuscripts, validated blind against expert reviews on 30 preprints.

At BIIE I own the design and build of a multi-agent LLM system for scientific peer review. Underneath it is a knowledge graph I built end to end, symbolic throughout so every claim stays traceable, with an ontology that breaks manuscripts into hypotheses, methods, experiments, results and claims, linked by typed relations including support and contradiction. The agents use it for multi-hop retrieval, claim verification, provenance and novelty checks, alongside our MCP tools for literature, database and figure analysis.

I built the evaluation framework as well: rubric-based LLM-as-judge scoring, critique matching against human reference reviews, and inter-rater reliability. The validation study was blinded and pre-registered, with a panel of 16 PhD domain experts.

Spiking networks and event-based vision

Gradient-based attribution for spiking networks that removes the main limits of earlier methods, tested with perturbation experiments at the pixel and spike level.

At Intel Labs I developed gradient-based feature-attribution methods for spiking neural networks trained with backpropagation, adapting Saliency and Integrated Gradients to event-camera and frame data. On CIFAR-10 and DVS128-Gesture, across neural codings, the methods identified that under 5% of spikes drive a full change in prediction, at two to three orders of magnitude less compute than LIME and RISE.

I trained the networks from scratch with surrogate gradients, ran models on Intel Loihi, and wrote the team's tutorial notebooks on neuromorphic models and training, which became part of the lab's documentation. Published as first author in Frontiers in Neuroscience.

Event cameras were also at the centre of my work for the Providentia++ programme: vehicle detection from cameras mounted on Autobahn gantries, co-calibration of the event camera with an RGB camera, fusion of their detections, and YOLO fine-tuned for night and heavy precipitation.

Vision on the edge and at scale

Detection, segmentation and anomaly detection running in customer plants on industrial PCs and Jetson, and a foundation model fine-tuned across 16 H100s.

At Siemens I developed and deployed edge computer-vision systems with HMI and robot integration across customer projects, including automated quality inspection in solar-panel manufacturing, using TensorRT, DeepStream and Triton.

In a small research team I co-led our work on applying foundation models to industrial anomaly detection and defect analysis, including fine-tuning SAM with LoRA. With one other research engineer I built and debugged the distributed training setup for it: 16 H100 GPUs on a multi-node Slurm cluster, DDP for data parallelism and DeepSpeed for sharding model states.
